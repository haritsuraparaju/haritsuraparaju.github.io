About

I started competing in science fairs in seventh grade, and each project has pushed me further into chemistry. What began as a question about hydrogen fuel cells turned into a broader interest in energy and sustainability: how to store it, how to make fuel from biomass, and how to turn a greenhouse gas into something useful.

Along the way I've learned how to design experiments from scratch, run titrations and electroplate electrodes, and analyze data until it actually says something. At the BioBuilder summer program I picked up a full set of molecular biology techniques, from PCR to protein purification, which opened up the biochemistry side of my interests.

Outside the lab I play piano and basketball, and I study Sanskrit.

Research

Three years, three projects

Each project grew out of the last. All three ask the same underlying question: how do you get the most useful chemistry out of a system?

Grade 9Electrochemistry · Catalysis

Optimizing nanoflower electrode catalysis for converting carbon dioxide into formate

I worked on an electrochemical cell that reduces CO2 to formate, a useful chemical feedstock, and focused on improving the catalyst on the electrode using nanoflower structures, whose high surface area gives more active sites for the reaction.

1st Place · Sanofi Award · WRSEF

Grade 8Biofuels · Biochemistry

Enzymatic vs. mechanical degradation of algae for ethanol production

Algae can be broken down and fermented into ethanol. I compared two ways of breaking down the algae, using enzymes and using mechanical methods, to find out which made ethanol production more efficient.

3rd Place · MSEF

Grade 7Energy · Electrochemistry

How electrolyte concentration affects the efficiency of a hydrogen fuel cell

I tested how changing the electrolyte concentration in a hydrogen fuel cell changes its efficiency, which was my first project working with electrochemical systems.
