About

About me

I'm curious and motivated, and I enjoy seeing a project through from an idea to a result. I began competing in science fairs in seventh grade with a question about hydrogen fuel cells. Each project led to the next, and my interest has grown into energy and sustainability: how to store energy, how to produce fuel from biomass, and how to turn a greenhouse gas into something useful.

Research has taught me persistence. Experiments fail and data can be messy, so I've learned to design experiments from scratch, run titrations, electroplate electrodes, and keep analyzing until the results are meaningful. At the BioBuilder summer program I learned molecular biology techniques such as PCR and protein purification, which expanded my interest into biochemistry.

Outside the lab I play piano and basketball, study Sanskrit, and was part of the team that won first place at the Worcester Regional MATHCOUNTS competition. Balancing these activities has taught me to plan my time carefully.

Research

Three years, three projects

Each project grew out of the one before. All three ask the same underlying question: how do you get the most useful chemistry out of a system?

Grade 9ELECTROCHEMISTRY · CATALYSIS

Optimizing nanoflower electrode catalysis for converting carbon dioxide into formate

I worked on an electrochemical cell that reduces CO2 to formate, a useful chemical feedstock. I focused on improving the electrode catalyst using nanoflower structures, whose high surface area provides more active sites for the reaction.

Key takeaway: small changes to a surface can have a large effect on performance.

1st Place · Sanofi Award · WRSEF

Grade 8BIOFUELS · BIOCHEMISTRY

Enzymatic vs. mechanical degradation of algae for ethanol production

Algae can be broken down and fermented into ethanol. I compared enzymatic and mechanical methods of breaking down algae to determine which made ethanol production more efficient.

Key takeaway: well-designed controls are what make a comparison trustworthy.

3rd Place · MSEF

Grade 7ENERGY · ELECTROCHEMISTRY

How electrolyte concentration affects the efficiency of a hydrogen fuel cell

In my first project, I tested how changing the electrolyte concentration in a hydrogen fuel cell affects its efficiency. This is where my interest in electrochemistry began.

Key takeaway: a clear, focused question matters more than complex equipment.
